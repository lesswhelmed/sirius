## I. The positioning statement (locked)

> **For** prosumers drowning in things they meant to keep, read, or act on —
> **Sirius** is the life-context engine that turns everything you capture into
> private, structured context about your life and uses it to close the loop.
> **Unlike** second brains that only store and recall, Sirius understands *why*
> something matters and increasingly acts on it — on your terms, with on-device
> privacy and full ownership.

**One line:** _Your life, in context._
**The promise:** _Feel lesswhelmed._

### Supporting taglines (reuse / remix)
- "Capture now. Sorted later."
- "Everything you capture, drafted into something worth keeping."
- "AI drafts it. You decide what's worth keeping."

## II. Broad-prosumer strategy: let segments self-select

Sirius is not pitched to one niche. The **hook is universal** — *capture anything,
get something worth keeping* — and the funnel lets each segment recognize itself.

```
            ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
   HOOK ──► │ "Capture anything. Get something worth   │
            │  keeping. On your terms."                │
            └─────────────────────────────────────────┘
                     │            │            │
        ┌────────────┘            │            └────────────┐
        ▼                         ▼                         ▼
  Overwhelmed pro          Second-brain power user      Creator
  "stop losing things"     "finally, context + own it"  "capture → publish"
```

- **Lead with the universal benefit**, not a persona. The overwhelmed professional
  feels relief; the power user hears "ownership + context layer + MCP"; the creator
  hears "capture → draft → publish."
- **Self-selection beats segmentation** for a broad prosumer tool: one product, one
  hook, three doors in. Channel and message *emphasis* shift per segment (below),
  the product does not.

## III. Personas

### Persona A — The Overwhelmed Professional ("Maya")

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Context** | Knowledge worker / manager. Tabs, screenshots, half-read links, voice memos to self. Saves things "to deal with later." Later never comes. |
| **Jobs-to-be-done** | "Get this out of my head and somewhere I trust." "Turn the pile into something I can actually use." "Stop the low-grade dread of forgetting." |
| **Pains** | Scattered across apps; nothing is structured; search fails because nothing was filed; guilt about the backlog. |
| **Trigger moments** | Inbox-zero attempts; a missed follow-up that mattered; end-of-week "what did I even do?"; a screenshot graveyard in Photos. |
| **Message that lands** | _"Capture now. Sorted later. Feel lesswhelmed."_ Relief and control, not features. |
| **Where to reach them** | Productivity / "second brain" YouTube and newsletters; LinkedIn; App Store search ("notes", "capture", "organize"); ProductHunt. |

### Persona B — The Second-Brain Power User ("Dev")

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Context** | Already runs Obsidian / Notion / Readwise. Cares about data ownership, structure, and tooling. Skeptical of cloud lock-in and AI hype. |
| **Jobs-to-be-done** | "Keep my own data and still get real AI structure." "Map my corpus by meaning, not folders." "Plug my context into the AI tools I already use." |
| **Pains** | AI features are bolt-on or cloud-locked; ownership and AI feel mutually exclusive; manual filing tax; no portable context layer. |
| **Trigger moments** | A privacy/lock-in scare with an incumbent; hitting the limits of folders/tags; discovering MCP and wanting to feed an agent *their* corpus. |
| **Message that lands** | _"Your context, owned — and usable by the AI you choose."_ Lead with on-device drafting, one-tap export, the Tree, and the MCP corpus server. |
| **Where to reach them** | Obsidian/PKM communities; Hacker News; MCP / AI-agent circles; r/ObsidianMD, r/PKMS; technical newsletters. |

### Persona C — The Creator ("Sol")

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Context** | Writes, posts, ships. Collects references, screenshots, links, clips as raw material; needs to turn input into output without a heavy workflow. |
| **Jobs-to-be-done** | "Capture inspiration the second I see it." "Turn raw input into a draft worth publishing." "Share some of it; keep the rest private." |
| **Pains** | The gap between collecting and publishing; reformatting by hand; no clean private→public path; tools that are either notes *or* a CMS, never both. |
| **Trigger moments** | Share-sheeting a link/image mid-scroll; a backlog of "content ideas"; wanting a public page without standing up a site. |
| **Message that lands** | _"Everything you capture, drafted into something worth keeping."_ Lead with the iOS Share Sheet, AI drafting you review, and the public catalog at `/catalog/[handle]`. |
| **Where to reach them** | Creator Twitter/X; TikTok/Reels on workflow; the Share Sheet as a viral demo ("share anything into Sirius"); creator newsletters. |

## IV. Category design — own "life context" + "close the loop"

Positioning is a fight over the words customers use to describe the problem. Sirius
should own two phrases and repeat them until the market borrows them back:

- **"Life context"** — the private, structured understanding of *your* aspirations,
  projects, and inputs. Not "notes," not "memory" (which the incumbents own and
  which means *their* silo). Life context is **yours, portable, and the reason the
  loop can close.** When people say "I want my AI to actually know my life," that
  sentence should resolve to Sirius.
- **"Close the loop"** — the move from storing to *acting*. Competitors store and
  recall; Sirius's arc is capture → context → action. Use it for the north star,
  honestly labeled: today Sirius closes the *front* of the loop (capture → draft →
  organize); the Insight and Automation engines that close the *back* are roadmap.

**Discipline:** every asset reinforces one category sentence —
**"Sirius is the life-context engine that closes the loop, on your terms."**
Never claim the automation north star as shipped. The brand earns the future by
being precise about the present.
